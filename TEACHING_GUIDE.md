# TMUA Teaching Guide — tutor only

Written once, used for **every** TMUA lesson. The lesson materials themselves
(`core_concepts.qmd`) are written to be **read aloud** — teach straight from them. This file holds
only the things that repeat from session to session and would be noise if copied into every lesson:
the session shape, the phrases, the pronunciation, and the prompts.

---

## The shape of a TMUA session (2 hours, one paper)

| Time | What |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:10 | The test, and the one number that matters: **3 min 45 s per question** |
| 0:10–0:50 | The core ideas of *this* paper — teach from `core_concepts.qmd`, in order |
| 0:50–1:35 | Guided walkthrough (`walkthrough_student.qmd`). **She drives.** You give the *cue*, never the answer |
| 1:35–1:55 | The questions flagged ⏱ 6 min+ — do these together |
| 1:55–2:00 | The four-box review method. Set the homework |

**Homework, every time:** the same paper again, **cold and timed (75 min)**, at least three days later —
long enough to have forgotten the answers, short enough to remember the cues. Then sort every question
into the four boxes (below).

**The four boxes** — the closing message of every session:

1. *Right and fast* — nothing to learn.
2. *Wrong, silly slip* — annoying, not a lesson.
3. ***Right but slow*** — **the most valuable box on this test.** She can already do it; she just paid
   six minutes for a three-minute question. Find the cue she missed.
4. *Wrong, no idea* — a technique to go and learn, then redo from blank.

> "Most students only ever look at the questions they got wrong. On the TMUA, that's half the lesson."

---

## The three things to say in every single lesson

These are the lesson. Say them until they are boring.

1. **"We're not looking for the answer. We're looking for the fastest honest route to the answer."**
   - "You could grind this out. It'd take six minutes. What's the shortcut?"
   - "Every question here has a slow way and a fast way. The exam is testing the fast way."

2. **"Read the options first. They're telling you something."**
   - "What do the options have in common? What's that telling you about the shape of the answer?"
   - "Can you kill three of them without doing any maths?"

3. **"Guess and move on."**
   - "A blank scores the same as a wrong answer — so a blank is just a worse decision."
   - "Leaving this one is the *right* call. Mark it, guess a letter, come back."
   - *This one is genuinely hard for good students. Make her practise it.*

## Prompts for the guided walkthrough

Your job during the walkthrough is to say the **cue**, never the answer. In rough order of how often
you'll need them:

- "What's repeating?"
- "Read the options. What's that telling you?"
- "What did the question actually **ask for**? Read the last line again."
- "You've found $u$. What *was* $u$?"
- "What's the range of that variable? Is it allowed to be anything?"
- "Is there a faster way? … There is. Look at the denominator again."
- "Sketch it. Don't solve it — sketch it."
- "Count the terms properly. Is it 90 or 91?"
- "Take your time — tell me when you're done."

**Do not** let her open the mark scheme mid-walkthrough. The distractor tables are the reward at the end.

## My go-to phrases — say it your way

**Starting**
- "Shall we begin?" · "Right — let's get started." · "Okay, let's dive in."

**Today's topic, and why it matters**
- "Today we're doing a whole paper — but not the way you'd sit it."
- "We're not here to get twenty right. We're here to find out how the setter thinks."

**Reassurance**
- "If anything's unclear, stop me — that's what I'm here for."
- "Please ask. A question now saves ten minutes later."
- "It's okay not to get it yet."

**Giving her time alone**
- "Take your time — tell me when you're done."
- "Have a go on your own first. I'll be quiet."
- "Try it. If you get stuck, that's useful information too."

**Doing one together**
- "Let's work through this one together." · "I'll do the first one, you do the rest."

**Practising until it's familiar**
- "Let's work through this example to **get the hang of** the method."
- "The steps will start to **feel familiar** after a couple."
- "Doing one more will help it **sink in**."
- *(Say "get the hang of it" / "get comfortable with it" — never "get yourself familiarized".)*

**Why the practice matters**
- "Once this becomes **second nature**, the hard questions get much easier."
- "The tough problems are just the same method with more steps."
- "This is the **foundation** — everything else builds on it."

**Ending warmly**
- "Well done — great effort today." · "That was a good session. See you next time."

**Singular → plural traps**
- **vertex → vertices** — VER-teks → VER-tih-seez *(not "vertex-es")*
- **axis → axes** — AK-sis → AK-seez
- **radius → radii** — RAY-dee-us → RAY-dee-eye
- **maximum → maxima** · **minimum → minima**
- **hypothesis → hypotheses** — hy-POTH-uh-seez
- **counterexample → counterexamples** *(one word, no hyphen)*

**Small words that trip people up**
- **squared** — SKWAIRD *(one syllable)* · **cubed** — KYOOBD *(one syllable)*
- **root** — rhymes with *boot*
- **inclusive** — in-KLOO-siv
- **iff** — say it as "**if and only if**". Never read it aloud as "iff"
