# TMUA Paper 2 — Eight-Year Trends, 2016 → 2023

Tutor only. Companion to [`PAPER2_TOPIC_MAP.md`](PAPER2_TOPIC_MAP.md), which classifies 2021–23 in detail.

**The question this file answers:** is 2016–2020 the same paper as 2021–2023?

**The answer is no.** The topics recur, but the *balance* has moved a long way. A course built on
2016–2018 papers prepares a student for a paper that no longer exists.

All 160 questions classified on the same two axes as the topic map. Answers are the official keys.

---

## 1 · The headline

**The share of Paper 2 that requires reasoning — as opposed to Paper 1 calculation — has risen from a half
to four fifths.**

| Sitting | 2016 | 2017 | 2018 | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Questions needing reasoning | 10 | 10 | 13 | 10 | 13 | 14 | 13 | **16** |
| **as a percentage** | 50 % | 50 % | 65 % | 50 % | 65 % | 70 % | 65 % | **80 %** |

Not a smooth climb — 2018 and 2020 already reached 65 % — but the floor has risen. **No paper since 2020
has been as calculation-heavy as 2016, 2017 or 2019 were**, and 2023 is the most reasoning-heavy paper in
the test's history.

---

## 2 · What moved

| Reasoning type | 2016 | 2017 | 2018 | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | **2016–20** | **2021–23** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pure calculation | 10 | 10 | 7 | 10 | 7 | 6 | 7 | 4 | **44 %** | **28 %** ↓ |
| Necessary / sufficient | 3 | 2 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 5 | 5 | 5 | **14 %** | **25 %** ↑ |
| Quantifiers / "possible" | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 4 | 3 | 2 | **5 %** | **15 %** ↑ |
| Spot the error | 1 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | **9 %** | **10 %** → |
| Converse · contrapositive · statement form | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 4 | **2 %** | **8 %** ↑ |
| Counterexample | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 2 | 1 | 1 | **9 %** | **7 %** → |
| Other "which must be true" | 1 | 1 | 5 | 2 | 4 | 1 | 1 | 2 | **13 %** | **7 %** ↓ |
| Puzzle / proof-ordering | 2 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 | **4 %** | **0 %** ↓ |
| | 20 | 20 | 20 | 20 | 20 | 20 | 20 | 20 | 100 | 60 |

### The three real movements

**1 · Calculation was squeezed out (44 % → 28 %).** In 2016, 2017 and 2019 *half the paper* was Paper 1
technique in disguise. A strong calculator could pass Paper 2 without ever naming an implication. That
route is closing.

**2 · Necessary/sufficient took the space (14 % → 25 %).** It went from 2–3 questions a paper to **exactly
5, three years running**. This is the single most dependable block of marks on the modern paper, and it is
where a course should start.

**3 · Quantifiers tripled (5 % → 15 %).** In every paper from 2016 to 2020 there was **exactly one**
quantifier question. From 2021 there are two to four. The $\forall\exists$ versus $\exists\forall$
distinction used to be worth one mark; now it is worth up to four.

### What did *not* move

**Spot the error and counterexample are stable** at roughly 2 and 2 a year across all eight sittings.
They are the constants of the paper — reliably present, never growing. Worth teaching early precisely
because they never change.

**"Which must be true" fell** (13 % → 7 %), but that is partly reclassification: the modern paper asks the
same idea in the sharper language of necessary/sufficient rather than the vaguer "which must be true".
2018 is the outlier, with five of them.

## 3 · Two corrections to `PAPER2_TOPIC_MAP.md`

Both claims in that file were made from three years of data and do not survive the other five.

### "Spot the error: exactly two per paper, every year"

Over eight years the count is **1, 3, 2, 1, 2, 2, 2, 2**.

| | 2016 | 2017 | 2018 | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| error questions | 1 | **3** | 2 | 1 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 |

The correct statement is **"two in most years, but between one and three"** — and the *positions* also
vary more than the 2021–23 window suggested (2017 had them at Q4, Q9 and Q18). Two is a good planning
assumption, not a guarantee.

### "'Only if' phrasing is new in 2023"

It is not new — it **came back**. Counts of "only if" in the question papers:

| 2016 | 2017 | 2018 | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *n/a* | 2 | 5 | 2 | 8 | **0** | **0** | **12** |

So the wording was standard through 2017–2020, **vanished entirely for two years**, then returned at
double its old frequency. Same for "if and only if" (2, 4, 2, 3 → 0, 0, **7**).

*The practical consequence is unchanged but better founded:* a student drilled only on 2021–22 will meet
"only if" for the first time in the exam. But so will a student who skipped 2017–2020 — and those papers
are where the phrasing can be practised.

**2016 cannot be counted here.** Its question-paper PDF has broken font encoding and extracts as garbage,
so every keyword count for 2016 would read zero. That is a **detection failure, not an absence** — the
2016 questions were classified from the official worked-answers PDF, which extracts cleanly.

## 4 · A finding that changes the toolkit: Prf4 *has* been examined

Throughout this project I recorded — in `toolkit/index.qmd`, in 이우성's hub and in my own notes — that
**no TMUA paper had ever used the Prf4 "scrambled proof" format**. That is **wrong**.

> **TMUA 2016 Paper 2 Q7** (answer **C**)
>
> *The four real numbers $a,b,c,d$ are all greater than 1. Suppose they satisfy
> $\log_c d=(\log_a b)^2$. **Use some of the lines given to construct a proof** that
> $\log_b d=(\log_a b)(\log_a c)$.*
>
> Nine numbered lines are supplied, and the eight options are orderings:
> **C** "(1). Then (3), so (5), so (9), so (7), and therefore (4), hence $(*)$ as required."

This is the Prf4 format exactly — pick the correct chain through a set of given lines. It has appeared
**once in eight years**, which is why the type felt absent, but "never examined" was a false statement and
S6 now has a genuine past-paper anchor.

A second, weaker instance: **2022 Q17** offers as options *"the student should have stated step II after
step III"* — proof-step ordering used as a distractor inside an error question.

**Consequence for teaching:** S6 stays low priority — one appearance in eight years — but it is no longer
speculative, and the claim in the materials must be corrected rather than repeated. (2022 Q17 was also
classified in the topic map as a plain spot-the-error; its options are really about the **converse** and
about **step order**, so it sits across Err and Arg1.)

## 5 · What stayed the same across all eight years

These are the genuinely stable patterns, now tested on 160 questions rather than 60.

**Divisors and primes, always wrapped in reasoning.** 2016 Q5 (counterexample), 2017 Q5 and Q9, 2018 Q17,
2019 Q4 and Q5, 2020 Q4, 2021 Q4 and Q10, 2022 Q3 and Q7, 2023 Q4, Q11 and Q16. *Never once a computation.*
Note the mid-period dip — "prime" appears 13 times in both 2017 and 2018, then 2, 1, 1 in 2019–21, then
climbs back to 8 by 2023.

**A plane shape asked as a logical condition, essentially every year.** 2016 Q9 (triangle congruence),
2018 Q5 (quadrilateral with perpendicular diagonals), 2019 Q10 (parallelogram — the rhombus),
2020 Q7 (parallelogram → square, rhombus as counterexample), 2021 Q18 (triangle), 2022 Q11 (kite) and
Q19 (polygon in a circle), 2023 Q9 (pentagon). **Eight sittings, eight appearances.**

**Q1–Q2 are calculation.** True in all eight papers, without exception — 16 of 16.

**The counterexample question sits early.** 2016 Q5, 2017 Q5, 2018 Q3, 2019 Q4, 2020 Q4, 2021 Q4,
2022 Q3, 2023 Q3. **Never later than Q5 in any of the eight papers.**

## 6 · What this means for a course now

1. **Weight the recent papers.** 2021–2023 are the only fair models of the current balance. 2016, 2017 and
   2019 are half calculation and will flatter a student's score. Use them for topic practice, not as mocks.
2. **Necessary/sufficient first.** It doubled, it is now exactly five marks a paper, and it is the most
   trainable thing on the test.
3. **Quantifier order is no longer a one-mark curiosity.** Budget real teaching time for
   $\forall\exists$ against $\exists\forall$.
4. **Mine 2017–2020 for "only if" practice.** That is where the phrasing lives; 2021–22 has none of it and
   2023 has twelve.
5. **Do not drop the constants.** Spot-the-error and counterexample have not changed in eight years — about
   four marks a paper between them, for a fixed and small amount of teaching.
6. **S6 stays last, but stop calling it hypothetical.** One real question exists: 2016 P2 Q7.

## Method and limits

Questions were classified from the official worked-answers PDFs in
`_resources/past_papers/<year>/`, cross-checked against the official answer keys; the 2021–23 rows agree
with the per-question table in `PAPER2_TOPIC_MAP.md` §6. Ligatures (`ﬃ`, `ﬁ`, …) were normalised before
any keyword counting — without this, "sufficient" reads as zero occurrences in the 2021 paper.

**Three limits worth stating.**
*One:* the 2016 question paper does not extract as text (broken font encoding), so its keyword row is
unavailable and its classification rests on the mark scheme plus page images.
*Two:* the boundary between "necessary/sufficient" and "other which must be true" is a judgement, and
some of the 13 % → 7 % fall in the latter is reclassification rather than a real change in the paper.
*Three:* 20 questions a year is a small sample; single-year movements (2017's three error questions,
2018's five "must be true") are noise. Only the five-year-block comparisons in §2 are worth acting on.
